<TryStep n={1} kind="predict" title="Ask a yes/no question" minutes={3} concept="brightness">

In the code:

- `light` is a pixel's brightness.
- `THRESHOLD` is a number you choose.

`light < THRESHOLD` asks one question: is this pixel darker than that number? Python answers `True` or `False`.

<Task slot="task">

<Theory slot="theory" title="Watch out" line="line 4" list="watch out: < is not ≤">

`120 < 120` is `False`: a pixel exactly at the threshold stays paper.

</Theory>

<Hint>`30 < 100` is `True`; `200 < 100` is `False`. Do the other two the same way.</Hint>
<Hint>The three darkest numbers are 30, 90 and 120. The threshold must be above 120 and no more than 200.</Hint>
<Hint kind="solution">`THRESHOLD = 150`</Hint>
<Hint kind="explain">

Picture a ruler from 0 (black) to 255 (white). Your four numbers sit on it: 30, 90, 120 and 200.

`THRESHOLD` is a mark on that ruler:

- left of the mark: `True`, gets ink
- on the mark or right of it: stays paper

Where must the mark go so that three numbers are on its left?

</Hint>

<TryStep n={3} kind="fix" title="The blue that never shows" minutes={3} concept="bands">

Three colours need two questions:

- below 90: blue
- from 90 to 149: pink
- 150 and up: paper

Python reads the `if`s from the top.

<Task slot="task">

This rule should give three colours: blue, pink and paper. No blue shows.

Change the order of the two `if`s so the blue appears.

</Task>

<Theory slot="after" title="Why it worked" line="lines 7 and 9" list="why it worked: narrow first">

<Diagram folder="/try-media/fig" name="if-order" ext="webp" phoneHeight={1230} alt="Two rulers. With the below-90 question first: blue, pink and paper bands, and a pixel at 10 lands in blue. With the below-150 question first: only pink and paper; the pixel at 10 stops in pink." caption="The same two `if`s in both orders. A pixel at 10 stops at the first `True`." />

In a chain of `if`s the first `True` wins, so the narrow question (below 90, fewer pixels) goes before the wide one (below 150, more pixels).

</Theory>

<Hint>A pixel at 10 is below 150, so the first `if` already returns pink for it. Which `if` should come first?</Hint>
<Hint>Check `light < 90` first, then `light < 150`. Keep the numbers.</Hint>
<Hint kind="solution">Move `if light < 90:` and its `return BLUE` above `if light < 150:`.</Hint>
<Hint kind="explain">

Picture two doors in a row, one for each `if`. A pixel walks through the first door that lets it in; `return` means it stops there.

1. The wide door (below 150) lets in every pixel under 150, the dark ones too.
2. So if it comes first, no pixel ever gets through the narrow door (below 90).

Which door must come first?

</Hint>
